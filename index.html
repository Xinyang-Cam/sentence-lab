03 / HOW DOES THIS WORK?

How does a computer compare two sentences?

First, how can a sentence become numbers?

Every sentence is turned into a list of 384 numbers, called an embedding. Think of it as giving each sentence its own location in a mathematical space. Sentences with related meanings often end up pointing in similar directions.

So, where does the similarity score come from?

The model compares the directions of the two embeddings using cosine similarity. When their directions are very close, the score approaches +1. At a right angle, it is 0. For opposite directions, it is −1.

What does the score actually tell us?

A higher score suggests that the model sees a closer relationship between the two sentences. But a high score doesn’t necessarily mean they express exactly the same meaning. Think of it as a useful clue, rather than a definitive answer.

Illustrations simplify the ideas. The live score, vectors and heatmap above are calculated from your own sentences.
